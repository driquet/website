On November 2, 2026, **[Gabriel Loiseau](https://gabrielloiseau.github.io/)** defended his PhD thesis, **Privacy and Utility-Preserving Transfer Learning for Text Anonymization**. Congratulations, Dr. Loiseau! 🎓

A PhD is a long road, and an industrial one (a CIFRE) adds a second set of expectations: the work has to hold up in front of a scientific jury and be useful to a company. Gabriel managed both, and I'm very proud to have supervised him alongside Marc Tommasi, [Damien Sileo](https://sileod.github.io/) and Maxime Meyer.

In Gabriel's words: "It was an honor to present this work to an outstanding jury. I'm deeply grateful to [Pierre Lison](https://nr.no/en/employees/pierre-lison/), [Aurélie Névéol](https://aclanthology.org/people/aurelie-neveol), [Ivan Habernal](https://www.trusthlt.org/) and [Claire Gardent](https://members.loria.fr/CGardent/) for their careful reading and for such a rich discussion."

## The thesis in a nutshell

Anonymizing text is not only about removing names. Style, context and rare details are often enough to tell who wrote a text or who it talks about, and aggressive rewriting destroys what made the text useful in the first place. Gabriel's thesis tackles that trade-off: how to rewrite text so that it protects people while staying useful for the task it was collected for, and how to measure both sides honestly.
