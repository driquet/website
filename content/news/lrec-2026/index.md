In May, I went to **LREC 2026**, the 15th Language Resources and Evaluation Conference, held in **Palma, Mallorca, Spain** from May 11 to 16, with two of my PhD students, **Senaid Popovic** and **[Gabriel Loiseau](https://gabrielloiseau.github.io/)**. Both had a paper to present.

Senaid presented **DIDECO** as a poster at the main conference. It is the first annotated dataset for detecting both explicit and implicit intents in digital communications: *what* an email asks for, and *how* it pushes the reader to comply.

> :uk: **DIDECO: An Annotated Dataset for Intent Detection in Digital Communications**. Senaid Popovic, **Damien Riquet**, Maxime Meyer, Fabien Lauer, Yannick Parmentier. LREC 2026. [\[link\]](https://hal.science/hal-05616045)

Gabriel presented our work on distilling the privacy-assessment abilities of a large language model into small encoder models (about 150M parameters) that stay aligned with human judgments, at the LEGAL / CALD-pseudo workshop co-located with LREC.

> :uk: **Distilling Human-Aligned Privacy Sensitivity Assessment from Large Language Models**. [Gabriel Loiseau](https://gabrielloiseau.github.io/), Damien Sileo, **Damien Riquet**, Maxime Meyer, Marc Tommasi. LEGAL / CALD-pseudo 2026. [\[link\]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29497)

On a personal note, this was my first academic conference in a long time, and in a different field from the one I did my PhD in. I was struck by how big research has become: the number of people, papers and topics. And from the look of it, it's only getting bigger by the hour.

Proud of both of them: presenting at a major conference is a milestone, and they did great.
